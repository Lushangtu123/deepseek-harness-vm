# Investment research

Use this mode for company, industry, market, valuation, portfolio, or investment-thesis analysis. It produces research support, not autonomous financial action.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer primary and authoritative sources:

1. Regulatory filings and official disclosures.
2. Company investor-relations materials and earnings transcripts.
3. Government, central-bank, and recognized statistical sources.
4. Reputable data vendors and clearly attributed secondary research.

Use current sources when freshness matters. Record publication date, reporting period, currency, units, and accounting or statistical basis for material figures.

## Analytical discipline

- Separate reported fact, management statement, market consensus, calculation, and inference.
- Reconcile non-GAAP measures to reported figures when material and possible.
- Make assumptions visible and keep calculations reproducible.
- For forecasts or valuation, provide base, bull, and bear cases when useful.
- Include the strongest counter-thesis, risks, catalysts, and explicit thesis-invalidation conditions.
- Mark unavailable, stale, estimated, or paywalled data honestly.

## Deliverable

Structure the result around the decision it informs. A full memo normally includes thesis, evidence, business and industry context, financial quality, valuation, scenarios, catalysts, risks, counter-thesis, monitoring indicators, and sources. Use a shorter structure for narrow questions.

When the user requests a full reusable memo, adapt the optional [investment-memo template](../assets/templates/investment-memo.md) and remove sections that are not supported by available evidence.

## Boundaries

- Do not imply fiduciary, legal, tax, or regulated advisory status.
- Do not execute trades, transfers, purchases, or investment-account changes.
- Do not state that an investment is safe or guaranteed.
- Treat personal financial data as sensitive and avoid reproducing it unnecessarily.
